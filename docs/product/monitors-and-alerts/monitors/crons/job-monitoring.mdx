Sentry's Cron Monitoring service can notify you and store a timeline of the following check-in events:

- **Missed check-in:** The job didn't check in within the check-in margin after its expected time. This can happen for various reasons, such as:
  - The job scheduler is misconfigured, skipped, or failed to initiate your job.
  - Network issues, such as an outbound firewall or an unstable connection.
  - An invalid request format.
- **Timed out check-in:** The job sent an in-progress check-in but didn't send a final check-in within its max runtime.
- **Failed check-in:** The job has reported its execution as unsuccessful.
- **Successful check-in:** The job has reported its execution as successful.

To learn how Sentry decides when a check-in is expected, missed, or timed out, see [How Cron Monitoring Works](/product/monitors-and-alerts/monitors/crons/how-it-works/).

To see information about your cron monitor, open [Cron Monitors](https://sentry.io/monitors/crons/) and click on your cron monitor's name.

## Cron Monitor Details

The details page shows a timeline of check-ins for each environment, the issues the monitor has opened, and a table of recent check-ins. The sidebar shows the monitor's schedule, failure and recovery tolerance, and its next and last check-in.

If Sentry couldn't process some of the monitor's check-ins, for example because of an invalid payload or a rate limit, a banner at the top of the page lists the errors.

## Multiple Environments

To monitor the same job in different environments, use a shared schedule to send check-ins for multiple environments to one monitor. Each environment will have its own status and set of check-ins.

### Alerting on Specific Environments

To only receive failing or missed monitor notifications for a specific environment or set of environments, scope your **[Alert](/product/monitors-and-alerts/alerts/)** sources and **environments** to match the check-in you care about, and use filters (for example on tags such as `monitor.slug`) where needed. See [Select a Source](/product/monitors-and-alerts/alerts/create-alerts/#step-1-select-a-source). For how the check-in `environment` is sent, see your SDK [environment configuration](/platform-redirect/?next=/configuration/environments/).

### Muting and Deleting Environments

To stop an environment from creating issues, select **Mute Environment** from its menu in the monitor's timeline. A muted environment still records check-ins. If you no longer run the job in an environment, select **Delete Environment** instead. Otherwise, Sentry keeps recording missed check-ins for it.

### Existing Limitations

- It's not currently possible to disable an individual monitor environment.

- It's not currently possible to see an aggregate view of a monitors' status
  across all environments. Each environment will appear as its own row in the
  monitor list.

## Ownership

Cron monitors can be assigned an owner as either a Team or specific Organization Member. When an owner is assigned to a monitor, new issues produced by failures of that monitor will be assigned to that owner.

Monitor owners also receive notifications when a monitor becomes broken. Learn more about [broken monitor notifications](/product/monitors-and-alerts/monitors/crons/troubleshooting/#why-was-my-monitor-environment-marked-as-broken-or-automatically-muted).

## Disabling Your Cron Monitor

You can disable a cron monitor from its details page. A disabled monitor stops recording check-ins, no longer creates issues for failed or missed check-ins, and doesn't count toward your cron monitor quota.

Note: Disabling your cron monitor won't stop the execution of your job or change the configuration of your job scheduler.

### Automatic Muting

If a monitor environment has been consistently broken for an extended period, Sentry may automatically mute it to reduce notification noise. Unlike disabling, muting does not stop billing. Learn more about [automatic muting and broken monitors](/product/monitors-and-alerts/monitors/crons/troubleshooting/#why-was-my-monitor-environment-marked-as-broken-or-automatically-muted).
